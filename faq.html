Frequently Asked Questions

Here are some questions people often ask about landslides.

A landslide is the movement of rock, soil or debris down a slope. It can happen suddenly or develop over time.

Landslides can be caused by heavy rainfall, earthquakes, erosion and changes in groundwater. These factors can make slopes unstable.

They are more likely to happen in mountainous or hilly areas, especially on steep slopes with unstable soil.

Warning signs may include new cracks in the ground, leaning trees or poles, unusual water flow and strange sounds coming from the ground.

Mountainous areas in states and regions such as Chin, Shan, Kachin, Kayin and Rakhine are at risk, especially after heavy rainfall. People living near steep slopes should be aware of possible warning signs and stay prepared.

Learn about the risks around your home, prepare an emergency bag, plan evacuation routes and follow weather updates.

The risk is higher during the monsoon season, which usually lasts from May to October. Heavy rainfall can make mountain slopes unstable, increasing the chance of landslides in vulnerable areas.

Move away from the landslide area if it is safe to do so. Follow official instructions, avoid affected roads and move to a safer location.

People can check weather forecasts and heavy rainfall warnings from Myanmar's Department of Meteorology and Hydrology (DMH). These updates can help people in hilly areas stay alert during periods of heavy rain.
