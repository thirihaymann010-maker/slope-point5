Know what to do.
Stay safer.

A landslide can happen quickly. Knowing how to respond before, during and after one can help protect you and the people around you.

01
BE PREPARED FOR THE UNEXPECTED

Every moment matters.

Landslides can happen with little warning, especially during or after heavy rainfall. It is important to know your surroundings, prepare an emergency plan and understand where to go if you need to leave.

02 / YOUR ACTION PLAN

What should you do?

Select each stage to learn how to prepare and respond to a landslide.

Always follow instructions from local authorities and emergency responders.

STAGE 01 / PREPARATION

Before a landslide

Prepare early so you know what to do if your area becomes unsafe.

Know your surroundings

Find out whether your home or school is near steep slopes, streams or places where landslides have happened before. Learn which areas are safer to reach.

Plan an evacuation route

Discuss an evacuation plan with your family. Know where to go, how to get there and where to meet if family members become separated. Have another route in case the usual road is blocked.

Prepare an emergency bag

Keep drinking water, basic food, a flashlight, a first-aid kit, necessary medicines, a charged power bank and important documents in a waterproof bag.

Follow weather updates

Pay attention to official weather reports, especially during periods of heavy or prolonged rainfall. Know how to receive local warnings and evacuation instructions.

Remember: Make sure everyone in your household knows the emergency plan, including children and older family members.

STAGE 02 / IMMEDIATE RESPONSE

During a landslide

Act quickly and avoid the path of moving soil, rocks and debris.

01

If you are outdoors

Move quickly away from the path of the landslide if you can do so safely. Head towards the nearest safe high ground in a direction away from the moving debris. Do not try to cross its path.

02

If you are inside a building

If a landslide is striking the building and you cannot leave safely, stay inside. Move to the highest floor you can safely reach and take cover. Follow emergency responders' instructions when possible.

03

If you are travelling

Stay away from roads affected by landslides. Never drive or walk across moving mud, water or debris. Follow road closures and choose a safe alternative route.

Do not wait if you are in immediate danger.

If it is safe to evacuate, move away from the danger and contact local authorities once you are in a safe place.

STAGE 03 / AFTER THE EVENT

After a landslide

The danger may not be over even after the ground stops moving.

Stay away from the affected area

Additional landslides may happen after the first one. Keep away from the slope and surrounding areas until authorities say they are safe.

Listen for official updates

Follow announcements from local authorities and emergency teams. Do not return home until you are told it is safe.

Avoid damaged power lines

Stay away from fallen electrical wires, damaged poles and broken utility lines. Report them to the relevant authorities.

Help others from a safe place

If someone may be injured or trapped, inform rescue teams of their location. Do not enter the affected area or attempt a rescue if it puts you in danger.

Remember: A damaged building or nearby slope may still be dangerous. Do not enter until it has been checked and declared safe.

IMPORTANT SAFETY REMINDER

Never assume the danger is over.

Landslides can happen without clear warning signs, and more movement may occur after the first slide. Stay alert and follow official instructions.

03 / SHARING IMPORTANT INFORMATION

What should you report?

If you notice signs of a possible landslide or see one happening, move to a safe place first. Share clear information with local authorities or emergency responders so they can understand the situation.

01

Where did it happen?

Give the location as clearly as possible. Mention nearby roads, buildings or familiar landmarks to help emergency responders find the area.

02

What did you notice?

Explain what you actually saw, such as moving soil, falling rocks, new ground cracks, blocked roads or damaged buildings. Do not go closer to check.

03

Does anyone need help?

Tell responders if you know that someone is injured, trapped or needs assistance. Share only information you can safely confirm.

Keep yourself safe first. Report the situation from a safe location. Never enter a landslide area to collect information or check for people.

04 / BEYOND YOUR HOME

Preparedness starts in the community.

Communities can become better prepared when neighbours share reliable information, plan ahead and support people who may need extra help during an emergency.

01

Share verified warnings

Share weather updates and safety announcements from official sources, such as Myanmar's Department of Meteorology and Hydrology (DMH) and Department of Disaster Management (DDM). Avoid spreading unconfirmed information.

02

Plan with your neighbours

Discuss nearby safe places, possible evacuation routes and how to share important updates. Make sure everyone knows where to get help during an emergency.

03

Support people who need help

Check that older people, young children and people with disabilities can receive warnings and understand the community's safety plan. Arrange support with them before an emergency happens.
