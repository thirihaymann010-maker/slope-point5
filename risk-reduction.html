01 / UNDERSTANDING RISK REDUCTION

Prevention starts
before the rain.

Safer communities begin with better land management.

Landslide risk reduction means taking steps to lower the chance of landslides and reduce the damage they may cause. This can include protecting natural slopes, improving drainage, making safer building decisions and planning land use carefully.

Communities in hilly and mountainous areas should understand which places may be at risk. Local authorities and residents can work together to monitor changes in the land and improve the way their surroundings are managed.

03 / ENVIRONMENTAL PROTECTION

Healthy slopes
matter.

Natural vegetation plays an important role in protecting the land. Tree roots help hold soil together, while plants cover the ground and reduce the effects of rainfall. Protecting forests and green areas can help lower the risk of soil erosion.

01

Protect existing trees

Keep forests and natural vegetation on hillsides instead of removing them unnecessarily.

02

Restore damaged areas

Support suitable tree planting and vegetation growth in areas where the land has been damaged.

03

Reduce soil erosion

Protect exposed soil and avoid activities that can weaken or damage natural slopes.

04 / MONITORING LAND CONDITIONS

Know what to
look out for.

Monitoring changes in the land can help communities notice possible landslide risks early. Residents should report unusual changes so that local authorities can check the area and take suitable action.

IMPORTANT

Warning signs do not always mean a landslide will happen. However, they should be taken seriously and reported to local authorities or relevant professionals.

LAND MONITORING GUIDE 04 SIGNS TO NOTICE
01

Cracks in the ground

New cracks in soil, roads or walls near a slope may show that the ground is moving.

02

Leaning trees or poles

Trees, fences or utility poles that begin to tilt may be a sign of movement beneath the ground.

03

Changes in water flow

New wet areas, leaking water or sudden changes in the flow of streams may be signs of changes underground.

04

Small soil movements

Small slides, falling rocks or unusual soil movement on a slope may indicate that the area is becoming unstable.



05 / COMMUNITY ACTIONS

What can communities do?

Communities can help reduce landslide risks through careful planning and by avoiding activities that make slopes less stable.

✓

DO

  • Protect forests and natural vegetation on hillsides.
  • Maintain drainage channels to help control rainwater.
  • Check land conditions before starting construction.
  • Report cracks and unusual changes in the ground.
  • Work with local authorities to improve land-use planning.
×

DON'T

  • Remove large amounts of vegetation from steep slopes.
  • Block natural drainage paths or water channels.
  • Build homes in high-risk areas without proper assessment.
  • Dig into or cut away steep slopes without expert advice.
  • Ignore reports of ground movement or slope damage.
